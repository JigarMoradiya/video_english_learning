Here's something cool! c, k, and ck all make the same /k/ sound!
Three spellings... one sound! Let's see how!
Listen: c-at... k-ite... du-ck. /k/, /k/, /k/! Same sound, three different spellings.
So how do we choose? It depends on where the sound is in the word!
We use c most of the time. At the start of a word, before a, o, or u, we use c — c-at, c-ot, c-up!
But there's one special rule! Before e and i, c says /s/, like in city. So when we hear /k/ instead of /s/, before e or i, we write k — k-ey, k-it, k-ing!
And at the end of a word, right after one short vowel, we use ck — two letters, but just one /k/ sound! du-ck, ro-ck, ki-ck!
Let's try some words! c: cat, cup, cot. k: key, kit, king. ck: duck, rock, kick!
Now it's your turn! How do we spell duck? c, k, or ck?
It's ck — because there's a short vowel right before it!
Remember: c before a, o, and u... k before e and i... and ck after a short vowel at the end. Easy trick, right?
