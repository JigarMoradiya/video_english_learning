This one is called Floss. It doubles letters at the end of a word.
This one is the C-K rule. It helps us choose the right spelling for the /k/ sound.

Then X, one letter hiding two sounds.

Six rules. Every single one is about a short vowel.
Today we'll learn the first two properly... Floss and C-K.
The other four are waiting for you in Part Two.
Let's start with Floss.
Look at these four words.
Off. Bell. Miss. Buzz.
Now look carefully at the end of each word.
Two Fs. Two Ls. Two Ss. Two Zs.
Every one ends with a doubled letter.
So why does English do that?
There's a reason... and it's a good one.
The rule has three parts, and all three must be true.
First, the word must have one syllable. That means one clap.
Clap with me.
Off... Bell... Miss... Buzz.
One clap every time.
Second, the vowel must be short.
Listen.
Off... short o.
Bell... short e.
Miss... short i.
Buzz... short u.
Third, the word must end with F, L, S or Z.
Only those four letters.
When all three parts are true... double the last letter.
Let's try some more together.
Cliff ends with F, so it takes two Fs.
Tall ends with L, so it takes two Ls.
Grass ends with S, so it takes two Ss.
Fizz ends with Z, so it takes two Zs.
Shell. Dress.
Both are doubled, and both follow the same rule.
You're picking this up quickly. Well done!
Now here's something interesting.
Listen to these sounds.
Fffff...
Lllll...
Sssss...
Zzzzz...
Did you notice?
You can stretch every one of those sounds.
The doubled letter gives your brain a clue.
It says, "The vowel before me is short."
Without that clue, you might try a long vowel instead.
So remember this.
Short vowel... double letter.
They belong together.
Now let's look at the other side.
Leaf. Feel. Tool. Pool. Sail.
Listen to the vowels.
Leaf... Feel... Tool...
Those are long vowels.
They say their own names.
Now look at the endings.
Only one F. Only one L.
No doubling.
The vowel decides.
Short vowel... double it.
Long vowel... don't.
Always check the vowel first.
There are three little traps.
Trap number one.
Only F, L, S and Z double.
Not C. Not K. Not P. Not T.
Trap number two.
Long words.
Listen.
Travel.
Clap it with me.
Tra... vel.
That's two claps.
The Floss rule only works on one-clap words.
So travel keeps one L.
The same with pencil.
Pen... cil.
Two claps... one L.
One clap... double it.
More than one clap... leave it alone.
Trap number three.
Look at this word.
Bee.
It ends with two Es, but that's a vowel team.
It's not the Floss rule.
Floss only works with F, L, S and Z.
Here's an easy way to remember it.
The word Floss follows its own rule.
One clap.
A short o.
And a double S.
Floss tells you exactly what to do.
Pretty clever, isn't it?
Now for our second rule.
This one is about the /k/ sound.
Here's the problem.
Three different spellings can make the same /k/ sound.
C can make it.
K can make it.
And C-K can make it too.
So how do we know which one to write?
It's not a guess.
There's a rule, and it depends on two things.
Where the /k/ sound is in the word...
and what letter comes next.
Let's take them one at a time.
First, the letter C.
Use C at the start of a word when the next letter is A, O or U.
Listen.
Cat.
The next letter is A, so we write C.
Cup.
The next letter is U, so we write C.
Here are some more.
Cod.
Cot.
Cab.
Cut.
Every one starts with C, and every one is followed by A, O or U.
But there's one special rule.
Before E and I, the letter C usually says /sss/.
Listen.
City.
The C says /sss/, not /kuh/.
So when we want the /k/ sound before E or I...
we write K instead.
Key.
Kit.
King.
So remember...
C before A, O and U.
K before E and I.
Now for the third part.
This is the rule that gives C-K its name.
When the /k/ sound comes at the end of a word...
and it comes right after a short vowel...
we write C-K.
Listen.
Duck.
Back.
Kick.
Rock.
Luck.
Pack.
Sock.
Block.
Check.
Thick.
Every one ends with C-K.
And every one has a short vowel before it.
That's one small rule...
used in lots of words.
Here's something really important.
C-K is two letters...
but it makes just one sound.
Not /kuh/... /kuh/.
Just one /k/ sound.
Listen.
Duck.
Du...ck.
One /k/ at the end.
Two letters...
One sound.
That's all C-K means.
Now let's look at some common mistakes.
Duck... is correct.
Dukk... is wrong.
Ducc... is wrong.
Duk... is wrong.
English never doubles C.
And it never doubles K for this sound.
Don't swap them around either.
Cat needs a C.
Kat is not an English word.
Here's an easy trick to remember.
Short vowel at the end?
Use C-K.
Think of a duck.
