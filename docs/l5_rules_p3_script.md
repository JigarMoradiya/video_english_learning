# L5 · Spelling Rules — Part 3 · script

**The letter X, and the tricky letter W**

The last part of Level Five. Part 1 covered Floss and C‑K, Part 2 covered `‑ng` and `‑nk`.
This one finishes the level and recaps all six rules.

One continuous take, same teacher. Target ≈ **8:30**.

---

## How this script is written

Complete sentences, in a teacher's speaking voice. Letters and sounds are named precisely
and never confused with each other:

* a **letter** is written — "the letter x"
* a **sound** is spoken — "the sounds /k/ and /s/"

---

## The rules, and how honest each one is

| Rule | What is taught | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **X** | the letter x is one letter that makes **two** sounds, /k/ and /s/ | true for every word used: fox, box, six, mix, fix, wax |
| **X is never doubled** | we never write `xx` | true |
| **W changes the a** | after the letter w, the letter a often says /o/ instead of /a/ | **a family to learn, not an always-rule** — see below |

**The W rule is deliberately hedged, and the script says so out loud.**

`w` + `a` says /wo/ in want, wash, watch, wand, swan and water — but **not** in wag, wax
or wax's friends. So the script teaches it as a small family the child should try first and
then check, exactly the way `oo` is taught in the shorts. Teaching it as a rule that always
fires would be teaching something false.

# THE SCRIPT

## 1 · WELCOME (0:00 – 0:35)

Welcome back!

Last time we learned about two pairs of letters that come at the end of words. We learned that the letters n and g make one sound together, and that the letters n and k make that same sound with a /k/ joined on the end.

You listened really carefully, and today we are going to finish Level Five.

We have two more things to learn. First we will meet the letter x, which is a very unusual letter. Then we will meet the letter w, which likes to play tricks.

Let's start.

## 2 · THE LETTER X (0:35 – 1:40)

I want to show you something surprising about the letter x.

Every other letter you know makes one sound. But the letter x is different. The letter x makes two sounds, all by itself.

Listen to this word. Fox.

Say it with me. Fox.

Now listen right at the end of that word. Fo… ks.

Can you hear it? There are two sounds hiding in there. There is a /k/, and then there is an /s/.

Say those two sounds with me. /k/… /s/.

Now say them quickly, one after the other. /ks/.

That is the sound the letter x makes. One letter, but two sounds joined together.

## 3 · WORDS WITH X (1:40 – 3:00)

Let's read some words that use the letter x.

Here is the first one. Fox. The fox has a bushy tail.

Here is the next one. Box. We put the toys in the box.

Now this one. Six. I can count to six.

Let's keep going. Mix. We mix the flour and the eggs.

And this one. Fix. Dad will fix my bike.

One more. Wax. The candle is made of wax.

Look at all of those words together. Every single one of them ends with the letter x.

And every single time, that one letter is doing the work of two sounds. Fo‑ks. Bo‑ks. Si‑ks.

You are reading those really well.

## 4 · WE NEVER DOUBLE THE X (3:00 – 3:40)

Now here is something important to remember about the letter x.

Do you remember the Floss rule from Part 1? We learned that some letters get doubled at the end of a word. We double f, l, s and z.

But we never, ever double the letter x.

There is no such word as f‑o‑x‑x. We just write one x, and that is always enough.

So if you hear /ks/ at the end of a word, write one x. Never two.

## 5 · THE TRICKY LETTER W (3:40 – 5:20)

Now let's meet our last letter of the level. This one is the trickiest of them all.

It is the letter w, and the letter w likes to change things.

Let me show you what I mean.

Normally the letter a says /a/. You can hear it in the word cat. Cat.

But watch what happens when we put the letter w in front of it.

Here is our word. Want.

Say it with me. Want.

Did you hear that? The letter a did not say /a/ this time. It said /o/. Want.

The letter w changed it.

Let's listen to some more words where this happens.

Wash. We wash our hands.

Watch. I watch the birds.

Swan. The swan swims on the lake.

Wand. The fairy waves her wand.

Listen to the vowel in every one of those words. Want. Wash. Watch. Swan. It says /o/ every single time, even though we write the letter a.

That is the trick the letter w plays.

## 6 · BE CAREFUL WITH THIS ONE (5:20 – 6:20)

Now I have to be honest with you about something, because this is important.

The letter w does not do this in every word.

Listen to this one. Wag. The dog can wag his tail.

That word has a w and an a, but the a still says /a/. Wag.

So this is not a rule that works every single time. It is a small family of words that you will learn to know by sight.

Here is what good readers do, and I want you to do the same thing.

When you see the letter w with the letter a next to it, try the /o/ sound first. Then ask yourself: is that a real word?

If it is, you were right. If it is not, try the /a/ sound instead.

That is not cheating. That is exactly how reading works.

## 7 · ALL SIX RULES OF LEVEL FIVE (6:20 – 7:40)

Now let's put the whole level together. There were six rules, and you have learned every one of them.

Rule one was the Floss rule. When a word has one clap and a short vowel, and it ends with f, l, s or z, we double that last letter.

Rule two was the c, k and c‑k rule. We use c before a, o and u. We use k before e and i. And we use c‑k at the end, after a short vowel.

Rule three was the letters n and g. They make one sound at the end of a word.

Rule four was the letters n and k. They make that same sound with a /k/ on the end.

Rule five was the letter x. One letter, two sounds.

And rule six was the tricky letter w, which changes the letter a into an /o/ sound.

Six rules. That is the whole of Level Five, and you have finished it.

## 8 · CLOSE (7:40 – 8:20)

I am really proud of you. Level Five is one of the hardest levels, and you have worked all the way through it.

Next time we will start Level Six together, and we will learn some brand new sounds.

Don't miss it!

If you enjoyed this lesson, please like this video and subscribe.

And practise all six rules in the English Learning app. It's free on the Apple App Store and Google Play.

See you next time!
