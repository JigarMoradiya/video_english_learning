**‑ng and ‑nk**

One continuous take, same teacher. Target ≈ **8:30**.

---

## How this script is written

Complete sentences, spoken the way a teacher actually talks to a class — not a list of
words with gaps. Every rule is stated in full, then shown, then practised.

**Letters and sounds are named precisely, and they are not the same thing:**

* a **letter** is what you write — "the letters n and g"
* a **sound** is what you say — "the sound /ng/"

So the script never says "the sound N-G". It says *"the letters n and g together make one
sound"*. Children who blur those two ideas struggle later, and it costs nothing to be exact.

---

## The rules, and why each one is safe to teach

| Rule | What is taught | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| **‑ng** | the letters `n` and `g` at the end of a word make **one** sound, `/ng/`, and the vowel before it is short | sing, ring, king, long, sung, bang |
| **‑nk** | the letters `n` and `k` make the same `/ng/` sound with a `/k/` joined on the end | bank, pink, sink, junk, thank, wink |
| **listening** | the only difference between them is that final `/k/` | ring/rink, sing/sink, bang/bank, sung/sunk are true minimal pairs |

Nothing here is a partial pattern. Every word used follows the rule stated.

---

## Voice rules (carried from Part 1)

1. Never more than two statements in a row without a *look*, *listen* or *you*.
2. Speak to the child in every section.
3. Praise after every group, not once at the end.
4. Don't answer every question instantly — leave the marked pauses.
5. One idea per sentence, but write **whole sentences**.

# THE SCRIPT

## 1 · WELCOME (0:00 – 0:35)

Welcome back, everybody!

In our last lesson we learned two spelling rules. We learned the Floss rule, where we double the last letter. And we learned when to use the letters c, k and c‑k for the sound /k/.

You worked really hard on those, and today we are going to learn something new.

Today we are learning about two pairs of letters that come at the end of words. They are the letters n and g, and the letters n and k.

Let's begin.

## 2 · THE SOUND /ng/ (0:35 – 1:40)

I want you to listen to this word very carefully.

Sing.

Now say it with me. Sing.

Listen to the very end of that word. Can you hear it? That last part says /ng/.

Here is the important thing. We write that sound with two letters, n and g. But when they sit together at the end of a word, they do not say /n/ and /g/ separately. They join together and make just one sound.

Say the sound with me. /ng/.

Did you feel that? The back of your tongue lifts up and touches the roof of your mouth. Try it once more. /ng/.

Well done. That is the sound the letters n and g make together.

## 3 · WORDS THAT END IN ‑NG (1:40 – 3:00)

Now let's read some words that end with those two letters.

Here is our first word. Sing. The child can sing a song.

Here is the next one. Ring. The bell goes ring.

Now this one. King. The king wears a crown.

Let's keep going. Long. That snake is very long.

And one more. Sung. We have sung that song before.

Look at all five words together. Every single one of them ends with the letters n and g.

And now I want you to notice something else. Listen to the vowel sound in the middle of each word.

Sing has a short i.
Long has a short o.
Sung has a short u.

Every one of those vowels is short. So here is our rule. When a word ends with the letters n and g, the vowel before them is a short vowel.

You are doing really well.

## 4 · THE SOUND /ngk/ (3:00 – 4:10)

Now we are going to change one letter, and I want you to listen to what happens.

Here is a new word. Bank.

Say it with me. Bank.

Listen right at the end again. Can you hear an extra sound? There is a little /k/ at the end. Bank.

We write that with the letters n and k. They make the same /ng/ sound as before, but this time a /k/ joins on at the end.

Say it with me. /ngk/.

Can you hear how your voice stops at the end? That is the /k/ doing its job.

## 5 · WORDS THAT END IN ‑NK (4:10 – 5:20)

Let's read some words that end with the letters n and k.

Here is the first one. Bank. We keep our money in a bank.

Next one. Pink. That flower is pink.

Here we go. Sink. We wash our hands in the sink.

And this one. Junk. That box is full of junk.

One more. Thank. We say thank you when someone helps us.

Look at them all together. Every one of these words ends with the letters n and k.

And listen once more to the vowels. Bank has a short a. Pink has a short i. Junk has a short u.

Short vowels again, just like before. That is very good listening.

## 6 · IS IT ‑NG OR ‑NK? (5:20 – 7:00)

Now here is the part I really want you to hear, because this is where children often get mixed up.

These two endings sound almost the same. There is only one difference between them, and it is that little /k/ at the end.

Listen very carefully to these two words.

Ring. Rink.

I will say them again. Ring… rink.

Only one thing changed. The second word has a /k/ on the end.

Let's try another pair. Sing… sink.

And one more pair. Bang… bank.

Now it is your turn. I am going to say a word, and I want you to listen for that /k/ sound at the end.

Here is the first word. Thing.

*(pause ≈ 2.5 seconds)*

Did you hear a /k/? No, you did not. So that word ends with the letters n and g. Thing.

Here is the next word. Think.

*(pause ≈ 2.5 seconds)*

That time there was a /k/ right at the end. So that word ends with the letters n and k. Think.

You are listening beautifully. That is exactly how you choose between them. Say the word out loud, and listen for the /k/.

## 7 · AN EASY WAY TO REMEMBER (7:00 – 7:45)

Here is an easy way to remember all of this.

Both endings come after a short vowel, and both of them come at the end of a word.

If you hear a /k/ at the end, you write the letters n and k.

If you do not hear a /k/, you write the letters n and g.

That is the whole rule. Your ears do the work, and your hand writes it down.

## 8 · LET'S REMEMBER (7:45 – 8:15)

Let's say what we learned today.

The letters n and g make one sound at the end of a word, and that sound is /ng/.

The letters n and k make that same sound with a /k/ joined on the end.

And both of them follow a short vowel.

You learned two new spelling patterns today, and you learned to hear the difference between them. That is real reading.

Next time we will finish Level Five together. We will learn about the letter x, which makes two sounds all by itself, and about the tricky letter w.
